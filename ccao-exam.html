CCAO Mock exam

60 questions · 120 minutes · pass at 720

Written to the thirty CCAO-F objectives at the published domain weights, and deliberately harder than the live exam. Seven organisations recur throughout — a UK insurer's claims operation, a retailer rewriting 30,000 product descriptions, a consultancy under client disclosure rules, an NHS-style trust writing to patients, a bank's regulated support desk, a SaaS company's research and product work, and a council making decisions about residents. Every item has two plausible finalists, and every option — right and wrong — carries an explanation of why.

Read the objective tag on each item Every question is labelled with its domain and its objective (for example Output Evaluation and Validation 2.3 Fact-checking techniques). When you get one wrong, that tag is the exact section of the CCAO documentation to go back to, and the matching drill in the exercises.

Study mode recommended first

Check each answer as you go and read the explanation immediately, while the question is still in your head. No time pressure. This is the mode that teaches — the four explanations per item are the material, not the score.

Exam mode simulation

120-minute countdown, no feedback until you submit — the same length and clock as the real thing. Use it once you have worked through study mode, to find out which distinctions survive time pressure.

Before you start

  • Select-2 items. 10 questions ask for exactly two answers, and they are marked. Both must be right to score. The two are usually complementary layers — one catching accuracy, one catching completeness — not two phrasings of the same idea. If your picks are near duplicates, one of them is wrong.
  • Every item has a near-miss. The distractors are drawn from the eight patterns in the distractor catalogue: re-prompting harder, cosmetic fixes, instructions as controls, self-verification, tier changes without a reason, abandoning the task, over-caution, and the appearance of a control. Learn to name the pattern and the elimination is instant.
  • The right answer is often not the safest one. Roughly a fifth of the items score over-caution as wrong: demanding clinical review of an opening-hours notice, or refusing a whole use case that only needed one control added. Read what the consequence of being wrong actually is.
  • No length tell. The correct option is never the longest and never the shortest in its item, and the answer key is balanced across A/B/C/D. Guessing strategies score at chance here.
  • Scenarios repeat. Seven organisations carry all 60 items. Read each one carefully the first time it appears; after that you can skim it.
  • Scoring. Scaled 100–1000 like the real exam. 720 passes, which is 42 of 60. Because this mock is harder than the live paper, aim for 850+ here before you book — and read the per-domain breakdown on the results page rather than the headline number.
  • Your progress is saved in this browser, so you can close the tab mid-attempt and come back. In exam mode the clock keeps running.
  • Keyboard: 1–4 to select, ←→ to move, F to flag, Enter to check in study mode.

How to read a stem under time pressure Find the last sentence first — that is what is being asked. Then look for the two facts the stem planted: a stated capacity limit, a policy, a consequence, a “the prompt has not changed”, a volume, a named regulator. Those are never decoration. If two options both look right, ask which one the planted fact was put there to rule out.
What these questions are and are not They are original items written to the domain and objective list published in the Claude Certified Associate — Foundations Exam Guide, and to documented Claude product behaviour.
