CCDV Mock exam

60 questions · 120 minutes · pass at 720

Written to the twenty-five CCDV-F skills at the published domain weights, and deliberately harder than the live exam. Eight engineering organisations recur throughout — a bank’s support agent under an iteration cap, a clinical extraction pipeline that must represent absence, a retailer doing cost arithmetic over 30,000 items, a logistics modernisation programme using Claude Code across nine engineers, an analytics team turning execute_sql into an MCP server, a media desk with a 340-line prompt, a multi-tenant SaaS facing injection and exfiltration, and a legal team running sixty-turn review sessions. Every item has two plausible finalists, and every option — right and wrong — carries an explanation of why.

Read the skill tag on each item Every question is labelled with its domain and its skill (for example Applications and Integration 2.3 Claude API mechanics). When you get one wrong, that tag is the exact section of the CCDV documentation to go back to, and the matching drill in the exercises.

Study mode recommended first

Check each answer as you go and read the explanation immediately, while the question is still in your head. No time pressure. This is the mode that teaches — the four explanations per item are the material, not the score.

Exam mode simulation

120-minute countdown, no feedback until you submit. Deliberately tighter than the real thing: the live paper gives you 120 minutes for 53 items, this one gives you 120 for 60. Use it once you have worked through study mode, to find out which distinctions survive time pressure.

Before you start

  • Select-2 items. 10 questions ask for exactly two answers, and they are marked. Both must be right to score. The two are usually complementary layers — one structural, one operational — not two phrasings of the same idea. If your picks are near duplicates, one of them is wrong.
  • Every item has a near-miss. The distractors are drawn from the twenty patterns in the anti-pattern catalogue: prose as enforcement, the cap as the control, self-reported confidence, buying capacity instead of managing it, a bigger model instead of a better prompt, retrying a non-retryable error, and so on. Learn to name the pattern and the elimination is instant.
  • The right answer is usually the structural one. A large share of items turn on the same distinction: an enforcement requirement belongs in code, a permission rule or a hook, while a preference can live in a prompt. When an option asks the model to police itself, it is almost always the distractor.
  • No length tell. The correct option is never the longest and never the shortest in its item, and the answer key is balanced across A/B/C/D (18/17/18/17 over the 70 correct slots). Guessing strategies score at chance here.
  • Scenarios repeat. Eight organisations carry all 60 items, and they are the same eight used in the exercises — so the context you built there transfers. Read each one carefully the first time it appears; after that you can skim it.
  • Scoring. Scaled 100–1000 like the real exam. 720 passes, which is 42 of 60. Because this mock is harder and tighter than the live paper, aim for 850+ here before you book — and read the per-domain breakdown on the results page rather than the headline number.
  • Watch the two-item domains. Claude Code (3.1%) and Eval, Testing and Debugging (2.6%) carry two items each. On the live 53-item paper they may carry only one or two, which means a single careless answer can cost you a whole domain. Do not skim them because they are small.
  • Your progress is saved in this browser, so you can close the tab mid-attempt and come back. In exam mode the clock keeps running.
  • Keyboard: 1–4 to select, ←→ to move, F to flag, Enter to check in study mode.

Item counts follow the published weights, so Applications and Integration carries the most questions here for the same reason it carries 33.1% of the real exam — a third of the paper is about designing and integrating an application, not about models.

How to read a stem under time pressure Find the last sentence first — that is what is being asked. Then look for the facts the stem planted: a stated iteration cap, a request volume, a token count, a price per million, a “the prompt has not changed”, a named stop_reason, a regulator, a number of engineers. Those are never decoration. If two options both look right, ask which one the planted fact was put there to rule out.
What these questions are and are not They are original items written to the domain and skill list published in the Claude Certified Developer — Foundations Exam Guide, and to documented Claude product behaviour. They are not recalled or leaked exam content, and no source claiming to hold verbatim exam items was used. The live exam is 53 items in 120 minutes; this mock is 60 in the same 120 minutes, which is a deliberate choice — more coverage of the small domains and more time pressure than you will actually face. The scenarios are invented; any resemblance to a real organisation is coincidental. A strong score here is evidence you understand the material — treat it as that, not as a preview of the live item pool.
