CCAR-P Mock exam

70 questions · 120 minutes · pass at 720

Written to the thirty-eight CCAR-P objectives at the published domain weights, and deliberately harder than the live exam. Six systems recur throughout — a hospital group whose clinical note has no standing until a clinician signs it, a wealth manager that must inform without ever advising, a marketplace with 40M SKUs and a fixed $150,000 pilot budget, a government programme delivering inside an accreditation boundary against a date it does not control, a software company running Claude Code across 120 engineers on one shared service account, and a freight operator whose 6,000-token prompt now contradicts itself. Every item has two plausible finalists, and every option — right and wrong — carries an explanation of why.

Read the objective tag on each item Every question is labelled with its domain and its objective (for example Integration 3.2 Analyse authentication and authorisation requirements). When you get one wrong, that tag is the exact section of the CCAR-P documentation to go back to, and it points at the matching drill in the exercises.

Study mode recommended first

Check each answer as you go and read the explanation immediately, while the question is still in your head. No time pressure. This is the mode that teaches — the four explanations per item are the material, not the score.

Exam mode simulation

120-minute countdown, no feedback until you submit. Deliberately tighter than the real thing: the live paper gives you 120 minutes for 63 items, this one gives you 120 for 70. Use it once you have worked through study mode, to find out which distinctions survive time pressure.

Before you start

  • Select-2 items. 10 questions ask for exactly two answers, and they are marked. Both must be right to score. The two are usually complementary moves — one that fixes the defect and one that fixes whatever produced it — not two phrasings of the same idea. If your picks are near duplicates, one of them is wrong.
  • Every item has a near-miss. The distractors are drawn from the patterns in the anti-pattern catalogue: a detective control offered where a preventive one was available, a metric that improves when the system fails, sampling something that gates display, extrapolating a measurement past the population it was taken on, orchestrating a lookup, and upgrading the model to fix a routing error. Learn to name the pattern and the elimination is instant.
  • The arithmetic in the stem is never decoration. A $0.11 per-SKU price against a $150,000 budget, 78% of sessions answerable from one lookup, 12 engineers producing 61% of usage, a 2.1% lift with an interval running from −0.4% to +4.6% — every number is there to eliminate an option. If two finalists survive your reasoning, do the sum.
  • No length tell. The correct option is never the longest and never the shortest in its item, and the answer key is balanced across A/B/C/D (20/20/20/20 over the 80 correct slots). Guessing strategies score at chance here.
  • Scenarios repeat. Six systems carry all 70 items, and they are the same six used in the documentation caseboxes and the exercises — so the context you built there transfers. Read each one carefully the first time it appears; after that you can skim it.
  • Scoring. Scaled 100–1000 like the real exam. 720 passes, which is about 49 of 70. Because this mock is harder and tighter than the live paper, aim for 850+ here before you book — and read the per-domain breakdown on the results page rather than the headline number.
  • Watch the small objectives. Domain 3 carries thirteen items across eight objectives, so three of them (3.6, 3.7 and 3.8) get a single question each, and the same is true of 2.5, 4.6 and 7.3. On the live paper the same thing happens, which means one careless answer can cost you a whole objective. Do not skim them because they are small.
  • Your progress is saved in this browser, so you can close the tab mid-attempt and come back. In exam mode the clock keeps running.
  • Keyboard: 1–4 to select, ←→ to move, F to flag, Enter to check in study mode.

Item counts follow the published weights, so Integration carries the most questions here for the same reason it carries 19% of the real exam — nearly a fifth of the paper is about tool boundaries, identity, retrieval and observability, not about designing on a whiteboard. Solution Design is the second-largest at 17%, and Developer Productivity is the smallest at 7% — five items you should still not give away.

How to read a stem under time pressure Find the last sentence first — that is what is being asked. Then look for the facts the stem planted: a stated 3-second p95, a budget with no extension this year, a signature that is the legal act, an index built by a job holding full read access, a commitment that is externally binding, an assessor queue outside the programme’s control. Those are never decoration. If two options both look right, ask which one the planted fact was put there to rule out — usually it is the one that would breach a stated constraint, or the one that detects a violation a boundary could have prevented.
What these questions are and are not They are original items written to the domain and objective list published in the Claude Certified Architect — Professional Exam Guide, and to documented Claude product behaviour. They are not recalled or leaked exam content, and no source claiming to hold verbatim exam items was used. The live exam is 63 items in 120 minutes; this mock is 70 in the same 120 minutes, which is a deliberate choice — fuller coverage of all thirty-eight objectives and more time pressure than you will actually face. The scenarios are invented; any resemblance to a real organisation is coincidental. A strong score here is evidence you understand the material — treat it as that, not as a preview of the live item pool.
