CCAR-F Mock exam

65 questions · 120 minutes · pass at 720

Written to the thirty CCAR-F task statements at the published domain weights, and deliberately harder than the live exam. Six organisations recur throughout — a retailer’s support agent that keeps refunding without verifying, a logistics platform with two confusable order tools and three MCP servers that disagree about money, a research platform whose coordinator assigned the same facet three times, a code-review pipeline producing 23 comments a pull request at 70% noise, an insurer running a “be conservative” triage prompt over 8,000 documents a day, and a platform team turning ad-hoc agent configuration into guarantees. Every item has two plausible finalists, and every option — right and wrong — carries an explanation of why.

Read the task-statement tag on each item Every question is labelled with its domain and its task statement (for example Agentic Architecture and Orchestration 1.4 Enforcement surfaces). When you get one wrong, that tag is the exact section of the CCAR-F documentation to go back to, and it points at the matching drill in the exercises.

Study mode recommended first

Check each answer as you go and read the explanation immediately, while the question is still in your head. No time pressure. This is the mode that teaches — the four explanations per item are the material, not the score.

Exam mode simulation

120-minute countdown, no feedback until you submit. Deliberately tighter than the real thing: the live paper gives you 120 minutes for 60 items, this one gives you 120 for 65. Use it once you have worked through study mode, to find out which distinctions survive time pressure.

Before you start

  • Select-2 items. 10 questions ask for exactly two answers, and they are marked. Both must be right to score. The two are usually complementary layers — one structural, one operational — not two phrasings of the same idea. If your picks are near duplicates, one of them is wrong.
  • Every item has a near-miss. The distractors are drawn from the twenty patterns in the anti-pattern catalogue: prose where a guarantee is required, fixing the downstream component for an upstream fault, buying context instead of managing attention, reformatting a bloated CLAUDE.md instead of splitting it, self-reported confidence as a routing signal, retrying a failure the input cannot fix, and so on. Learn to name the pattern and the elimination is instant.
  • The right answer is usually the structural one. A large share of items turn on one distinction: a requirement that must hold every time belongs in code, a tool schema, a dispatcher precondition, a hook or an allowed-tool list — while a preference can live in a prompt. When an option asks the model to police itself, it is almost always the distractor.
  • No length tell. The correct option is never the longest and never the shortest in its item, and the answer key is balanced across A/B/C/D (19/19/19/18 over the 75 correct slots). Guessing strategies score at chance here.
  • Scenarios repeat. Six organisations carry all 65 items, and they are the same six used in the documentation caseboxes and the exercises — so the context you built there transfers. Read each one carefully the first time it appears; after that you can skim it.
  • Scoring. Scaled 100–1000 like the real exam. 720 passes, which is about 45 of 65. Because this mock is harder and tighter than the live paper, aim for 850+ here before you book — and read the per-domain breakdown on the results page rather than the headline number.
  • Watch the small task statements. Domain 5 carries nine items across six task statements, so three of them (5.3, 5.4 and 5.6) get a single question each. On the live paper the same thing happens, which means one careless answer can cost you a whole task statement. Do not skim them because they are small.
  • Your progress is saved in this browser, so you can close the tab mid-attempt and come back. In exam mode the clock keeps running.
  • Keyboard: 1–4 to select, ←→ to move, F to flag, Enter to check in study mode.

Item counts follow the published weights, so Agentic Architecture and Orchestration carries the most questions here for the same reason it carries 27% of the real exam — more than a quarter of the paper is about choosing an architecture and putting the guarantees in the right place, not about writing prompts.

How to read a stem under time pressure Find the last sentence first — that is what is being asked. Then look for the facts the stem planted: a stated $500 ceiling, a failure count like 47 of 12,000, a percentage that must be zero, a 900-line configuration file, a “the diff still fits in context”, a named stop_reason, a next-morning deadline, an archive that could not be reached. Those are never decoration. If two options both look right, ask which one the planted fact was put there to rule out — usually it is the one that would still leave a failure rate where the stem demanded a guarantee.
What these questions are and are not They are original items written to the domain and task-statement list published in the Claude Certified Architect — Foundations Exam Guide, and to documented Claude product behaviour. They are not recalled or leaked exam content, and no source claiming to hold verbatim exam items was used. The live exam is 60 items in 120 minutes; this mock is 65 in the same 120 minutes, which is a deliberate choice — fuller coverage of all thirty task statements and more time pressure than you will actually face. The scenarios are invented; any resemblance to a real organisation is coincidental. A strong score here is evidence you understand the material — treat it as that, not as a preview of the live item pool.
